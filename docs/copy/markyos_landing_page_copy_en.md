**Conventions used below**

- `[CHIP: …]` a small status label shown next to a feature
- `[CTA →]` a button
- `[VISUAL: …]` a direction for design, not copy
- `[PLACEHOLDER: …]` content that must be filled with real data before publishing (never publish it empty or invented)
- `[CONFIRM: …]` a fact to check before publishing
- *Why it works* notes explain the copy decision and the principle behind it. They're for you and the designer, not the page.

## 1. Strategy Foundation

Copy is the last step of strategy, not the first. Everything on the page traces back to these decisions.

### 1.1 Positioning

> **For studios:** For brand studios and freelance designers in the Arab world and Asia who lose weeks to scattered tools and hand over their best work as a PDF, Markyos is the bilingual brand platform that runs the whole project, from the first discovery question to a live brand hub the client owns. Unlike stitching together forms, whiteboards, Drive folders and a PDF, every step feeds the next, and the finished brand is handed over as a living site, not a zip file.
>
> **For companies:** For marketing managers and brand leads who are tired of being the human "is this on-brand?" checkpoint, Markyos is the brand hub that holds the strategy, the assets and the voice in one place, in Arabic and English, and checks the company's content against that voice in seconds. Unlike a PDF on a drive plus a per-seat writing tool that doesn't know the brand, Markyos knows *this* brand, and the whole company can use it.

**Category:** Brand hub (bilingual). We never call it a guideline viewer, a document tool, a design tool or a CRM.

**The enemy we name:** Brand decay. The slow drift that happens when a brand lives in a PDF, a Drive folder and people's memories.

**The core idea, in one line:** *Studios build it. Companies run it.*

### 1.2 Who's reading, and what they already know

Using Eugene Schwartz's five levels of awareness:

| Visitor | Awareness level | What the page must do first |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Studio owner or freelancer | **Problem-aware.** Knows the handoff is messy and the PDF gets ignored. Doesn't know a single platform exists for the whole project | Name the pain they already feel, then show the path |
| Marketing manager / brand lead | **Problem-aware, sometimes solution-aware.** Knows about brand portals and writing tools, doesn't know one tool can do both, in Arabic | Name the bottleneck, then show the tone checker fast |
| A client sent by a studio | **Product-aware.** They've seen a hub already | Make the Company path and pricing easy to find |

Because most visitors are problem-aware, the page leads with a promise and moves to the problem quickly, instead of opening with features.

### 1.3 The two jobs (Jobs to Be Done)

| | Studio | Company |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Functional job | Win, run and deliver brand projects faster | Keep the brand consistent across every department, channel, language and agency |
| Emotional job | Look more senior. Feel proud of the handoff | Stop being the bottleneck. Stop worrying about what goes out |
| Social job | Be seen by clients as a strategic partner, not a file supplier | Be the person who made the brand "work" across the company |
| "Aha" moment | The first AI brief analysis. The first auto-built guideline site | The first tone check of their own content, against their own voice, in Arabic |

### 1.4 Frameworks applied, and where

| Framework | Where it shows up on the page |
| :--- | :--- |
| **AIDA** (Attention, Interest, Desire, Action) | The page's overall arc: hero → problem → paths → proof → pricing → final CTA |
| **PAS** (Problem, Agitate, Solve) | Section 4.4 "Brands don't die. They decay." |
| **StoryBrand** (customer is the hero, brand is the guide) | Copy speaks to *you* and your client; Markyos is the guide with a plan (the step paths), never the hero |
| **Value Equation** (dream outcome × likelihood ÷ time × effort) | Every step card names the outcome, removes effort ("drop the zip, we'll sort it"), and cuts time ("a few clicks") |
| **Hick's Law** | The hero offers exactly two choices, matching the sign-up fork: studio or company |
| **Commitment and consistency** (Cialdini) | Visitors self-identify in the hero, and the sign-up screen asks the same question, so the click continues a decision already made |
| **Reciprocity** | A free plan that does a complete job, plus the first AI analysis free |
| **Authority** | Founder note; questions written by a strategist, not machine-translated |
| **Loss aversion** | "Brand decay" frames the cost of doing nothing as something lost, not something missed |
| **Specificity bias** | Real numbers from the product: 8 template looks, 16 sections built automatically, 19 layouts, 10-part analysis |
| **Anchoring** | Company pricing is set against what per-seat writing tools cost a five-person team |
| **Von Restorff effect** | One recommended plan per side, visually lifted |
| **Risk reversal** | Free plan, no card [CONFIRM], hubs stay live on the free plan, no surprise AI bills |
| **Peak-end rule** | The page ends on the strongest emotional line, not on the footer |

### 1.5 Voice for this page

**We are:** clear, warm, confident, specific, a little bold.
**We are not:** hype-driven, technical, cute, corporate.

- Short sentences. One idea each.
- "You" and "your client" more than "we".
- Real nouns over adjectives: "the old logo in the sales deck" beats "inconsistent brand assets".
- No exclamation marks. No "revolutionary", "seamless", "leverage", "unlock".
- Arabic is never an afterthought. Where a line shows bilingual work, show it in both scripts.

### 4.4 The problem: brand decay

**Eyebrow**
> The quiet problem

**Headline**
> ## Brands don't die. They decay.

**Body**
> You spend months getting a brand right. The strategy, the logo, the colours, the voice. Then it goes out into the world as a PDF.
>
> Six months later, sales is using the old logo. HR writes like a different company. The Arabic version says something the English never meant. And every "is this on-brand?" question lands on one person's desk.
>
> Nobody did anything wrong. The brand just had nowhere to live.

**Three pain cards**

| Card | Headline | Line |
| :-: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | The PDF nobody opens | Guidelines get emailed once, saved somewhere, and never seen again. |
| 2 | Two languages, drifting apart | Arabic and English versions get written by different people, at different times, with different words. |
| 3 | One person, every question | The marketing manager becomes the brand's help desk, and the bottleneck. |

**Transition line**
> It's not a people problem. It's a home problem.

*Why it works:* This is PAS. The problem is the drift; the agitation is three scenes the reader has lived through, written as specific images instead of abstract claims. "Nobody did anything wrong" removes blame, so the reader agrees instead of getting defensive. Naming the problem "brand decay" gives it a handle, and it's framed as loss (something you already had, slipping away), which motivates more than a missed gain. The transition line reframes the problem so the solution can only be a *place*.

### 4.7 For studios: the project path

**Eyebrow**
> For studios

**Headline**
> ## From the first question to the final handoff. In one place.

**Subheadline**
> Every step feeds the next one. Skip any step you don't need, or start from the middle with a brand you've already designed.

---

#### Step 1 · Discover

**Send a brief your client will actually finish.**

> Bilingual discovery forms your client answers at their own link, in Arabic or English, without making an account. Start from a template for a new brand, a rebrand or a sub-brand, then add, remove or reorder questions from a library written by a brand strategist, not a translation tool. Paste your call notes in too, so nothing said on Zoom gets lost.

*Small print:* Forms and responses are free and unlimited on every plan.

---

#### Step 2 · Analyze

**Get a senior strategist's read on the answers. The first one's free.**

> Markyos reads the brief and your call notes and gives you a structured report: goals, audience, competitors, personality signals, and the two things a junior designer would miss — the **contradictions** in what the client said, and the **questions you still need to ask**. It ends with two or three strategic routes the brand could take.
>
> Every finding lands as a draft. You accept what's useful. Nothing goes into the strategy without you.

*Small print:* One free analysis in every project, on every plan. Re-runs use credits.

*Why it works:* This is the studio's "aha" moment, so it gets the strongest benefit framing. "A senior strategist's read" sells the outcome (looking more senior to the client), not the feature (an AI report). Naming the two most valuable sections (contradictions, gaps) makes the promise concrete. "The first one's free" is reciprocity and removes the price question at the moment of highest curiosity.

---

#### Step 3 · Strategize (optional)

**Write the strategy where the brand will live.**

> Vision, mission, purpose, positioning, personas, voice traits, values and competitors, in both languages. Accept the analysis drafts you agree with, write the rest yourself, and the strategy flows straight into the guideline site later. Doing visual identity only? Skip it.

---

#### Step 4 · Explore

**Find the direction with your client, not just for them.**

> Collect references on a moodboard and Markyos pulls the palette out of the real pixels in your images. Group them, cull them, and show the client a direction they can react to.

`[CHIP: Coming spring 2027]` **Real-time whiteboard.** Sketch directions with your team and your client live, on the same canvas, with your approved logo, colours and type as live pieces. Clients join from a link, no account needed. And AI art direction turns a board into mood words, a palette rationale, and Arabic and Latin type pairings.

*Why it works:* What exists today (moodboard, palette extraction) is sold as fully available; what's coming is clearly marked and sits in the same card, so the visitor sees the direction of travel without being misled. "With your client, not just for them" names the emotional upgrade: collaboration instead of presentation.

---

#### Step 5 · Design

**Keep designing in the tools you love.**

> Markyos isn't a design tool, and it doesn't want to be. Design the identity in Figma, Illustrator or whatever you use. Everything you need to design well (the brief, the analysis, the strategy, the boards) is one tab away.

*Why it works:* Saying what the product is *not* builds trust and removes a fear ("will I have to change how I design?"). It's also true to the scope: no design tool, no AI-drawn logos.

#### Step 6 · Deliver

**Drop the zip. We'll sort it.**

> Drag in the final folder. Markyos opens it in your browser, finds the logos (and guesses which is primary, icon, mono or reversed), groups the fonts into families and checks which ones cover Arabic, pulls the colours, and files the rest. Then it shows you one screen — *"We found 6 logo files, 2 font families and 9 colours"* — so you can confirm and move on.

#### Step 7 · Build

**Your guideline site, built in a few clicks.**

> Pick one of 8 looks. Markyos assembles the site from what the project already holds: the logo system with clearspace and misuse examples, the colour section with a contrast check, typography in Arabic and Latin, imagery, and the strategy you accepted. Sixteen sections build themselves when their data exists. Then edit anything, with 19 layouts and a full builder, on desktop, tablet and phone.

*Why it works:* The value equation in one card: dream outcome (a finished site), effort removed ("a few clicks", "build themselves"), time cut. The numbers (8, 16, 19) make the claim believable.

> Invite your client, and the hub becomes theirs. Their own brand site, live and editable, with their own login. You keep the role you agreed on, the project stops counting against your plan, and your slot is free for the next client.
>
> No zip file. No "final_FINAL_v3.pdf". A brand they can actually use.

*Why it works:* This is the emotional peak of the studio story: pride in the handoff. The "final_FINAL_v3.pdf" line is a shared joke every designer recognises, which builds rapport through identification.

### 4.10 Tone checker spotlight

**Eyebrow**
> The tone checker

**Headline**
> ## Is this on brand? Know in seconds. In Arabic and English.

**Body**
> Paste any piece of content, pick the channel, and Markyos checks it against *your* voice, your department rules and your term base. Not a generic "friendly" or "formal" setting. Yours.

**What you get back**

> **A score out of 100**, with a word — On brand, Close, or Off brand
>
> **A score for each voice trait**, with the reason in one line
>
> **Flagged sentences**, with the rule or trait each one breaks
>
> **Term problems** — a banned word, a product name translated wrong
>
> **Arabic register mismatches** — Egyptian dialect on a channel set to Gulf, for example

**Two more things it does**

> **Rewrite in brand voice.** Keep the meaning, fix the voice.
>
> **Translate in brand voice.** Arabic to English and back, following your term base and the register of the channel. Transcreation, not word-for-word.

### 4.19 FAQ

**Headline**
> ## Questions, answered

**Who is Markyos for?**
> Two kinds of people. Studios and freelance designers who make brands for clients, and companies (usually a marketing manager or brand lead) who run their own brand. You choose which one you are when you sign up.

**I'm a designer and I also run marketing at a company. Do I need two accounts?**
> No. One login can have both a studio workspace and a company workspace. Switch between them from the menu in the top corner.

**Is Markyos a design tool?**
> No. Keep designing in Figma, Illustrator or whatever you use. Markyos is where everything around the design lives: the brief, the strategy, the exploration, the guideline site, and the brand after it's handed over.

**Does the AI design logos?**
> Never. AI in Markyos reads, compares and drafts words. It doesn't draw logos or invent brand lettering, and everything it suggests waits for a person to accept it.

**What happens to my client's hub if they never pay?**
> It stays live and editable on the free company plan, with up to 2 editors, a Markyos web address, and a small "Made with Markyos" badge. They upgrade when they want their whole team in, their own domain, and no badge.

**What counts as an "active project"?**
> A project you can still edit. Once you hand it over to the client or archive it, it stops counting, and the slot is free again.

**What's the difference between an editor and a viewer?**
> Editors can change the brand. Viewers can see it, download approved files and check their own writing. On paid company plans, viewers are free and unlimited.

**How good is the Arabic, really?**
> Arabic isn't a translation layer in Markyos. The interface mirrors fully, both scripts have their own logo and font slots, and the tone checker understands the difference between Modern Standard, Gulf, Egyptian and Levantine Arabic. The discovery questions were written in both languages by a brand strategist.

**I already have brand guidelines as a PDF. Can I bring them in?**
> Yes. Drop your brand files in and Markyos builds the hub from them. `[CHIP: Coming 2027]` Soon, it will also read your old PDF and draft the guideline sections, voice and key terms from it.

**Is my content safe?**
> Text you run through the tone checker isn't stored unless you choose to save it, and saved text is deleted after 30 days. Nothing in your workspace goes public until you publish it, and you can protect a published hub with a password.

**Can I use my own domain?**
> Yes, on paid plans. Password protection is available too.

**How do I pay?**
> By card, through Stripe, in US dollars. Cards that only work on the Mada network aren't supported yet.
